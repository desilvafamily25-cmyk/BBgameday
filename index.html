Why "no zone" matters

✕ ZONE (illegal)
Each defender guards an area: "you stand at the left of the key". Players stay in spots no matter where attackers go.
✓ PLAYER-TO-PLAYER
Each defender guards a person: "you've got number 7". If 7 runs to the corner, you go to the corner.

In plain English: before every defensive possession, each of your five should be able to point at one opponent and say "mine". Helping a teammate for a second and then going back to your own player is fine. Standing in the key guarding nobody is not — referees will warn you and can penalise it.

20-point rule

When a team leads by 20 or more, that team must retreat behind the three-point line as soon as the opposition gains possession — no pressing.

Does not apply during grading rounds 1–6.

Match-day rules

RuleWhat it means

Rules that change your tactics

No zone

Teach MATCH. Before the ball is inbounded, each girl points to her opponent. Help is a quick step, then back.

No timeout final 3:00 of 1st half

Need one in the first half? Call it before the clock shows 3:00.

Clock runs in timeouts

A timeout costs real game time (except in the final 2:00). Keep it to 30 seconds.

Clock stops final 2:00

Every whistle stops time. Make subs at those stoppages and keep a timeout for a key late possession.

5-second key

Taller girls can post up close to the basket a little longer — but keep teaching "in and out".

Size 6 ball & 3-pt line

Long shots are hard at U12. Layups win games — pass and cut to get them.

4 players / walkover

Confirm attendance the night before. Have parents' numbers in your phone.

20-point rule

20+ up? Tell your girls before it happens: "When they get the ball, we run back inside the 3-point line."

Coach IQ

The no-zone rule helps you: it forces every girl to learn to defend, not just stand. Player-to-player is also simpler to coach — one word: MATCH.

Chapter 4 · Team

Your Six-Player System

Type each name once — it appears everywhere in the app. Saved

These are game-day jobs, not permanent labels. Every girl still shoots, passes and defends. Swap the jobs around at training and from week to week.

Where they start in 5-out

How do I choose who does what?
  • Player 1: the girl who stays calm with the ball and looks up before she dribbles. Not necessarily your fastest or best shooter.
  • Player 2: your next most confident ball-handler. When 1 is resting, 2 brings the ball up.
  • Player 3: a girl who loves to run — she'll cut to the basket all game.
  • Player 4: your most willing defender — put her on their best player.
  • Player 5: tallest or toughest — she boxes out and finishes close to the basket.
  • Player 6: fills any job. She starts on the bench only because someone has to — tell her that.
Coach IQ

At U12 D level, reducing turnovers and getting defensive rebounds matters more than complicated plays. Pick your 1, 2 and 5 carefully — they control those two things.

How the plan works

  • Always a ball-handler: only one girl rests at a time, so player 1 or player 2 is always on court.

Chapter 6 · Offence

The Only Offence You Really Need

5-OUT: five spots outside the 3-point line. That's it.

The five spots

SpotExactly where
TopMiddle of the court, a big step behind the 3-point line.
Wings ×2Level with the free-throw line, outside the 3-point line.
Corners ×2In the corner, feet behind the line, near the sideline.

Rule of thumb: stay about 4 big steps (3–4 m) from the nearest teammate.

Four words run the whole offence

1 · Space

Stand on a spot, outside the line, facing the ball. If a teammate is closer than 4 steps, move away.

2 · Pass

Pass to the nearest open teammate. Chest or bounce pass — no lobs over defenders.

3 · Cut

Straight after passing, sprint to the basket with a hand up for the ball.

4 · Replace

When a spot is empty, the nearest player fills it. Keep all five spots full.

Why 5-out works at U12

  • Creates big gaps to drive and cut into
  • Fewer collisions and fewer tie-ups
  • Shorter, easier, safer passes
  • Every girl handles the ball and learns every spot
  • Stops five children crowding under the basket
When a teammate drives toward you, move away — slide to the corner or drift behind her — so she has a clear passing lane to you.
Coach IQ

Watch the players away from the ball. If the four without the ball are on their spots, your offence is working — even if the shot misses.

Chapter 7 · Offence

Pass, Cut & Replace

The one action your team runs all game. Step through it.

Sideline cue"PASS AND CUT!"
  1. PassPlayer 1 passes to the wing.
  2. CutStraight away she sprints to the basket, hand up, eyes on the ball.
  3. ClearNo return pass? She keeps running through and out to the side with space.
  4. ReplaceThe nearest teammate fills the empty spot. The ball goes back to the top and we go again.
✕ Common mistake
Pass, then stand still and watch. Her defender relaxes, and the girl with the ball has nobody to pass to.
✓ Fix it
"Your job after you pass is to CUT." Praise every hard cut — even when the ball doesn't come.
Teach it at training in 5 minutes

Five girls on the five spots, no defence. Pass, cut, replace — keep the ball moving for 10 passes, then add a layup. Next week add two defenders.

Coach IQ

The cut works even without the ball: a girl who cuts hard drags her defender away, leaving more space for everyone else.

Chapter 8 · Offence

Dribble Rules for Beginners

Catch the ball, then in this order:

Look→Pass→Dribble

Dribble with a reason. If she can't name the reason, she should look to pass first.

✓ Good reasons
  • Move the ball up into open space
  • Beat a defender to the basket
  • Escape pressure
  • Attack the basket for a layup
✕ Bad reasons
  • Bouncing while thinking
  • Dribbling into teammates
  • Crossing into traffic
  • Picking it up too early with nowhere to go

If she's stuck

Stop→Pivot→Protect→Look
  1. StopTwo-foot jump stop. Pick the ball up strong.
  2. PivotKeep one foot glued to the floor; step the other foot away from the defender.
  3. ProtectBall under the chin, elbows out. Never hold it out in front.
  4. LookEyes up for the closest teammate. Teammates: cut toward her!
Sideline cue"PIVOT!"
Coach IQ

Don't over-coach the player holding the ball. She has a defender in her face already. Coach the four without the ball: "Cut to help her!"

Chapter 9 · Transition

Fast Break & Transition

The easiest baskets in U12 come in the 5 seconds after a rebound.

Rebound↓Chin / protect↓Outlet pass↓Ball in the middle↓Two run wide↓Attack if advantage↓If not: settle 5-out
Sideline cue"WIDE! WIDE!"

Three running lanes

Left lane

A runner sprints along the left sideline to the corner, then cuts to the basket.

Middle

The ball. Dribble up the middle; stop at the top of the key if a defender is waiting.

Right lane

A runner sprints along the right sideline, then cuts to the basket.

Why five up the middle fails: one defender can guard three girls at once, passes get deflected, and players run into each other. Wide runners stretch the defence so a simple pass makes a layup.

Attack or settle?

More of us than them? (3 v 2, 2 v 1)
↓
ATTACK — layup or pass to the open runner
Same numbers or more of them?
↓
SETTLE — fill the 5-out spots, then pass & cut
Coach IQ

The outlet is where turnovers happen. Teach your ball-handlers to run to the sideline at the free-throw line and call for the ball — never to stand in front of the rebounder.

Chapter 10 · Defence

Legal Player-to-Player Defence

Five defenders, five opponents, five jobs. No zones — ever.

In every diagram O = our players and X = opponents. Gold dotted lines show who is guarding whom.

Sideline cue"MATCH!"

Every defender's six jobs

  1. Find your playerAs soon as we lose the ball, find the girl you're guarding.
  2. Point & callPoint at her and call her number: "I've got 7!"
  3. Stay betweenStand between your player and the basket — always.
  4. See player AND ballTurn so you can see both without turning your head far.
  5. Move your feetWhen she moves, you move. Slide, don't reach.
  6. RecoverIf you help a teammate, go straight back to your own player.

How to set matchups

  • Match by size first, then speed. Tallest on tallest.
  • Put your best defender (often player 4) on their best scorer.
  • At the first dead ball, check: "Everyone point to your player!"

Staying legal — help without becoming a zone

✓ Helping

A quick step toward a driver to slow her, then straight back to your own player. You still have a player the whole time.

✓ Sagging

If your player is far from the ball, take a step or two toward the basket — but stay in line with her and keep seeing her.

✕ Guarding an area

Standing in the key guarding nobody, or "you take that side" — that's a zone. Not allowed.

! Switching & trapping

Don't teach switching or traps at D grade. If two defenders end up on one girl, the closer one shouts "I've got 7!" and the other finds the free player immediately.

Coach IQ

At the start of every opposition possession, glance at your five. Can each one point at her opponent? If not, call "MATCH!" — that single word fixes most U12 defensive problems.

Chapter 11 · Defence

Defensive Stance & Ball Pressure

Contain first. Steal second.

✓ Do

  • Knees bent, sit like on a low chair
  • Chest up, eyes on her belly button
  • Feet wider than shoulders
  • Hands active — one up, one low
  • Small, quick slides
  • About one arm's length away

✕ Don't

  • Cross your feet (unless sprinting to catch up)
  • Reach for the ball every time she dribbles
  • Stand straight up
  • Jump at fakes
Sideline cue"FEET!"

Contain the drive

Ball pressure is still player-to-player. The defender on the ball guards her own player tightly; everyone else stays with theirs. Pressure ≠ everyone running at the ball.
Coach IQ

Praise the slide, not the steal. "Great feet, Mia — you kept her out of the middle!" teaches the habit that wins more possessions than gambling for steals.

Chapter 12 · Defence

Help, Recover & Don't Ball-Watch

Every defender watches two things.

MY PLAYER
+ BALL

Teach the "two pistols": one hand points at her player, one hand points at the ball. If she can point at both, she's in the right spot.

Teammate beaten↓Step toward the drive↓Slow the ball↓Recover to MY player
Sideline cue"SEE BOTH!"
Keep it legal: help is a quick step, not a new home. Never tell a defender to "stay in the key" or "guard that side" — every defender always has one opponent.
Coach IQ

Watch the players away from the ball. Most easy layups against U12 teams come from a defender turning her head to watch the ball while her player cuts behind her.

Chapter 13 · Defence

Rebounding Wins U12 Games

Most U12 shots miss. The team that gets the ball wins.

Shot→Find→Contact→Box→Ball
Don't chase the ball first. Find your opponent, put your body between her and the basket, hold for a second — then go get the ball.
Sideline cue"BOX!"

When WE shoot

  • Player 5 (and the girl opposite the shot) attack the rebound.
  • Players 1 and 2 sprint back toward half-way — no easy fast break for them.
  • Everyone else: step in, then get back if they rebound.
Coach IQ

Count defensive rebounds for one half. If we get fewer than they do, half-time change #1 is "BOX!" — it's usually the fastest way to win more possessions.

Chapter 14 · Special situations

Simple Inbound Plays

Two plays. That's all you need.

Priorities

  1. Get it in safelyA safe pass beats a clever pass.
  2. Create separationReceivers cut hard — walk her in, sprint back out.
  3. Pass to the strongest safe receiverUsually player 1. That's the SAFETY.
  4. Flow into 5-outOnce the ball is in, fill the spots.

Baseline "BOX" — call it "BOX!"

  • Player 2 (good passer) takes it out.
  • Four girls stand in a box: two at the free-throw line ends, two beside the key near the basket.
  • Slap the ball = GO. 5 cuts to the basket, 4 pops to the corner, 3 runs to the wing, 1 comes back as SAFETY.

Sideline "GET OPEN"

  • Player 1 V-cuts: walk the defender toward the basket, then sprint back to the ball with hands up.
  • Player 3 comes back toward half-way as the SAFETY.
  • Others stay spread so there's room.

Guarded tightly?

  • Inbounder: stand a step back from the line so you can see everyone. Fake a pass to move the defender's hands.
  • Receivers: V-cut — take her in two steps, then sprint back out to the ball. Hands up, call your name.
  • Count in your head: 5 seconds to inbound. By 3, throw to the SAFETY.
  • Still nobody? If you have a timeout and it's allowed, use it before a 5-second violation.
  • Never throw a lob across the court. Bounce or chest pass only.
Coach IQ

Name the safety before every inbound: "1 is safety!" Knowing where the easy pass is calms the inbounder down more than any play.

Chapter 15 · Game management

Sideline Coaching Language

One cue. One correction. Then let them play.

✕ Too much"Shenara go left, switch, pass, move, defend her, no…"
✓ One word"SPREAD!" or "MATCH!"

The six sideline words

Secondary cues

Praise the decision

✕ Only outcomes
"Yes! Great shot!" (only when it goes in)
✓ Behaviours
"Great decision — that was the right pass." "Love that box-out!"
  • Use her name first, then the word: "Ava — FEET!"
  • Say it once, loud and calm. Don't repeat it five times.
  • Never yell at a referee. The girls copy you.
Coach IQ

Children can process about one instruction while running. A paragraph from the sideline gets heard as noise — and makes the girl look at you instead of the game.

How to Read the Game

See the problem → name the cause → one fix.

The 4-question scan (every few minutes)

  1. Are we spread on offence?
  2. Is everyone matched on defence?
  3. Are we boxing out?
  4. Who looks tired?

What's going wrong? tap a problem

Coach IQ

Fix the most common problem, not the most recent one. One bad turnover is noise; three in a row is a pattern worth a timeout.

Chapter 17 · Game management

Timeout Masterclass

2 per half. Each one is precious — and the clock keeps running.

Call one when…

  • Several turnovers in a row
  • The team is panicking
  • A defensive mismatch keeps hurting you
  • Players have forgotten spacing
  • The opponent has a big run / momentum
  • An important late-game possession
  • You need to reorganise subs or matchups
Don't call a timeout to tell off one player, or after a single mistake. And none allowed in the final 3 minutes of the 1st half.

The 30-second timeout

10s

The cue word, then "Let's go"

Ready-made scripts

Crowding the ball"Big breath. We're crowding the ball. Spread back into five-out — pass and cut. Let's go."
Turnovers"Breathe. We're dribbling into trouble. Stop, pivot, look for the closest teammate. Let's go."
Easy layups against us"Water, eyes on me. They're getting layups. Point to your player — MATCH — and get back. Let's go."
Losing rebounds"Good effort. They're getting second shots. Shot goes up — find, BOX, then ball. Let's go."
Coach IQ

Because the clock keeps running during timeouts (until the final 2 minutes), a long timeout steals playing time from your own girls. 30 seconds is plenty.

Chapter 18 · Game management

Half-Time + Second-Half Adjustments

Two minutes. Not a lecture.

Adjustment decision tree

Spacing?Girls bunched near the ball → "SPREAD" — 5-out spots
Turnovers?Dribbling into traffic → "PIVOT" — stop, protect, pass
Shots?Long, rushed shots → "CUT" — pass and cut for layups
Transition?Layups before we're back → 1 & 2 sprint back on every shot
Matchup?One girl scores everything → best defender on her; "MATCH"
Rebounds?Second and third shots → "BOX" on every shot
Maximum TWO changes. If you give three, they'll remember none.
Coach IQ

Start with what went right. Girls who feel they are doing well play the second half with more confidence — and listen better to the one change.

Close game? Stay calm, keep it simple.

Legal ball pressure when trailing

"Increase pressure" means each girl guards her own player closer — up toward half-way, an arm's length away, hands active. It does not mean two or three girls running at the ball and leaving players free.

20-point rule

If you lead by 20 or more, your team must retreat behind the 3-point line when the opposition gets the ball (not in grading rounds 1–6). Use it as a chance to play your newest girls and practise half-court defence.

Coach IQ

Late in a close game, the best thing you can say is short and calm: "Next play. MATCH." Your voice tells them whether to panic.
